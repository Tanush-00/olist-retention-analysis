What this tells us

  • Growth came from more orders, not bigger baskets: average order value stayed between R$147 and R$174 every month.
  • Revenue stopped growing in 2018. Once acquisition flattens, retention is the next growth lever.
  • The market is concentrated in the South-East (SP, RJ, MG = about 62% of revenue).

What this tells us

  • 97.8% of customers buy only once. Fewer than 0.5% of a cohort return in the following month.
  • Repeat customers spend about 2x as much over their lifetime, so +1 point of repeat rate ≈ R$150k in revenue.
  • Target High-Value New customers with a second-purchase offer within 90 days, and run a win-back campaign for At Risk - High Value.

Repeat rate by first-order review

Customers whose first order earned 5 stars return most often. First orders Jan-2017 to Feb-2018.

What this tells us

  • Olist promises about 24 days but delivers in about 12.6. Still, 6.8% of orders arrive late, and those average 2.27 stars vs 4.29.
  • A late first order goes with a lower repeat rate (2.93% to 1.99%, -32%), but the gap shrinks to -21% (p = 0.08) when every customer gets the same follow-up time, so treat it as suggestive. Delivery protects ratings; on its own it does not create loyalty.
  • Fix the peaks (capacity planning before Black Friday), add proactive delay alerts, and serve the North-East with regional carriers.

What this tells us

  • Revenue is highly concentrated: the top 10% of sellers bring about two-thirds of it. Keeping them happy is critical.
  • The 30% of sellers with the worst late rate cause 54% of late orders. A seller scorecard with SLA targets would hit most delays.
  • Big categories like bed/bath/table and furniture are rated below average. Check product descriptions and packaging there.
